Commission Plan Stress Test: the agreed plan

The commission plan agreed on November 20, run against our real costs, sale by sale. Each bar is one sale as a share of its price: what it costs us, what the Specialist is paid, card fees, and what is left for ads, overhead and profit. The top bar is the board draft as presented (commission only); the bottom bar is the agreed plan.

What was agreed

10% on everything a Specialist sells, with three exclusions.

  • One rate. 10% on first plans, second intensives, Continuing Care and equipment, whether equipment comes in a package or on its own.
  • Never commissioned: labs, physician or clinical-consult time (Dr. Jared, Dr. Kyle or any licensed clinician who diagnoses), and supplements. Inside a package they come off the price before the 10%.
  • Supplements fund the care team instead. An amount equal to 10% of supplement revenue (a placeholder; 5% was also discussed) goes into a quarterly PT and PTA pool, paid on team markers. Not at launch: it starts once there is cash for it.
  • Returning families. A second intensive is built from hours only, with no kit, and pays 10% of its price.
  • Paid on cash. Commission accrues when the sale is made and is released when the money is collected.

The care team pool

PTs and PTAs are never paid on revenue, sessions, supplements or equipment. They share a quarterly pool earned as a team.

How it pays

  • Three markers: family satisfaction (NPS) of 80 or higher; clean compliance audits with notes complete; 90% of families complete their plan of care and move into Continuing Care.
  • Payout: all three markers pay 100% of the team's share, two pay 75%, one or none pay nothing.
  • Split: each PT and PTA team's share follows its caseload; within a team, 60% to the PT and 40% to the PTA.
  • Unearned money stays in the pool for later quarters. The company announces each quarter's amount.
  • Start: not at launch. The board sets a small first pool once there is cash, and raises it as the company grows.

How big it gets

10% of supplement revenue, paid quarterly.

Two things to settle before this goes in the plan

Size. Supplements are only inside the Pre-Intensive and the v1 plans, and the meeting agreed resold supplements carry little margin. At the deck's $1,000 a month, the pool is $300 a quarter, a tenth of the draft's $3,000. The meeting's $100,000-a-month figure would take supplement sales roughly a hundred times larger than the model shows.

Who recommends the supplements. The PTs and PTAs whose pool grows with supplement sales are the ones who recommend supplements. The meeting agreed they cannot be commissioned on supplements; a pool sized by supplement revenue can look like the same incentive one step removed, whether or not the team is told where the money comes from. The November 19 review recommended sizing the pool by completed plans of care instead. Put this question to the attorney before launch.

Not decided in the meeting

These are in the board draft but were not discussed. The final deck holds them out of the plan until the board decides.

Plan B: $45,000 base plus a percentage

The meeting worked only the commission-only plan.

If Plan B stays, it needs a rate on the same net basis. At 10% commission-only, a base-plus plan would pay roughly 4% on top of the base.

The monthly stretch goal (17% / 12% after $125,000)

Set when the base rate was 15%. It no longer lines up with a flat 10%.

Drop it, or reset it relative to 10% (for example 12% after the goal).

Bonuses: $250 bundle, 1.5% paid in full, $1,000 top Specialist

Equipment now earns 10% in or out of a package, which does the bundle bonus's job.

Recommend dropping the bundle bonus; decide the other two.

Who else is in the pool

An appointment scheduler was mentioned but not decided.
