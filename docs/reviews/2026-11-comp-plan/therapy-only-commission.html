Scenario: commission on therapy and coaching only

Specialists are paid only on the part of a sale that is therapy or coaching. The kit, labs, supplements, Dr. Jared's time and the NeuroSage Hub carry no commission, whether they are bundled into a program or sold on their own.

Bottom line

At the plan's 15% rate, the change cuts what a Specialist earns on a program by 7 to 14% compared with the amended plan, and the company keeps the difference: about $170 per Clinical family and $140 per Wellness family. It also ends commission on stand-alone kit and supplement sales, so nobody is paid to push devices.

If the board wants Specialist pay to stay roughly where it is, raise the first-plan rate to 17% of the therapy amount. Pay then lands within about 1% of the amended plan on a typical month, and the incentive still points only at therapy and coaching.

2. How each program is broken into line items

Each program is broken into the line items it is built from in the pricing model: every block of PT, coordinator and coach time, each device in the kit, each lab, the physician hour, each supplement and each Hub month. The price is then divided among those lines:

  1. Non-therapy lines are valued at what they cost us. These are the kit and devices, labs, Dr. Jared's time, and supplements and supplement credits. They are mostly pass-through, so none of the margin is assigned to them.
  2. The Hub is valued at its $50 monthly list price. It has no recorded cost.
  3. Everything left is the therapy amount. It is split across the therapy and coaching lines in proportion to their list rates: PT $240 an hour, coordinator and coach $100 an hour. The split only shows where the dollars sit; it does not change the total.
  4. Commission is the rate times the therapy lines only. First plans pay 15%, or 17% for the pay-neutral option; continued plans pay 10%. Non-therapy lines show a dash.

Every program's lines add up exactly to its price, so a commission statement can be checked line by line.

7. What the board is choosing between

Therapy-only at 15%: margin first

  • The company keeps about 1.2 points of price on Clinical and 2 points on Wellness. Against the pricing review's 20% EBITDA target, that is a meaningful share of the gap.
  • Specialists take a pay cut of about 11%. Expect pushback, and possibly attrition, from the best closers.

Therapy-only at 17%: incentive first (recommended)

  • Pay is roughly unchanged, so the change is easier to roll out.
  • Commission follows the part of the sale the company actually earns its margin on. Device upsells no longer pay, which also lowers the compliance risk of selling equipment to families.
  • It costs about 1% more than the amended plan on a typical month.

With either rate, keep these amendments: the 25%-of-gross-profit cap per sale (A2), the full-window clawback (A6), and commission on cash net of fees (A7). Drop the bundle-bonus rule (A3), because bundled equipment no longer pays at all.
