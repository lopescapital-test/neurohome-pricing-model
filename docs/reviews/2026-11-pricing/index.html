Pricing and margin review: building room for a 20% EBITDA

A pricing-consultant pass over the catalogue in this repo's pricing model. It asks how much each program has to leave after delivery cost for marketing, overhead and profit to reach margins investors would compare with digital-health and therapy peers. It then sets out the price, cost and scope changes that close the gap.

Bottom line

The core programs show 65 to 72% gross margin in the model, but closer to 58 to 60% once employment cost, kit shipping, kits lost on early exit and financing fees are counted. After the amended commission, that supports an EBITDA of only about 13% if marketing runs 15% of revenue and overhead 13%. That is on par with Hims & Hers (13.5%) and U.S. Physical Therapy, and well short of the 20% target this review proposes.

One package of changes gets Clinical and Guided to 62 to 65% gross margin and about 18 to 20% EBITDA, without cutting what families see as the core of the program. The package has four parts:

  • a 5 to 7% list-price increase on Clinical, Guided and Wellness, tested before rollout;
  • three kit fixes;
  • financing terms that put interest on the patient instead of the merchant;
  • moving eight late-program Clinical sessions from PT to PTA.

The Pre-Intensive needs a separate fix: it leaves 20% before marketing today, which is not enough to run as a stand-alone product.

3. Recommendations, ranked by dollars per family

Each figure is for Clinical, 60-minute sessions, unless stated otherwise, and assumes the change is made alone. "Margin" is gross margin on the realistic basis.

R1. Raise Clinical and Guided list prices about 6%, and Wellness about 5%

+$725 per Clinical family+2.4 pts marginOwner: leadership

The new prices would be:

  • Clinical 60: $14,500 → $15,400
  • Clinical 90: $18,400 → $19,500
  • Guided 4-day 60: $10,850 → $11,500
  • Guided 5-day 90: $13,450 → $14,250
  • Wellness 2×60 quarter: $6,600 → $6,900

Of the $900 Clinical increase, $725 stays after commission and fees. The increase pays off unless it costs more than about 11% of enrolments (15% once a $2,000 ad cost per family is counted; see section 5). The value story already supports it: the kit retails at $2,960 and the labs at $1,050, both included.

To test it, quote the new prices to new leads only, for six weeks, and compare close rates with the prior six weeks before rolling out.

R2. Clinical delivery: move eight late-program sessions from PT to PTA, or taper weeks 7 and 8

+$300 to $350 per family+2.1 to 2.4 ptsOwner: clinical lead

There are two options:

  • PTA days. A PTA leads two of the five weekly sessions in weeks 5 to 8, under the PT's plan of care. Families still get 40 sessions, and capacity grows by eight PT hours per family.
  • Taper. Weeks 7 and 8 drop to three sessions a week, framed as a step toward running the home program on their own.

This is a clinical decision, and the clinical team should make it. Check each state's PTA supervision rules for telehealth first. Do not go further than this: a Clinical program with less PT starts to look like Guided at a higher price, which undermines the premium.

R3. Financing: offer patient-paid-interest terms, and let 0% plans run only on short terms

+$145 per family−1 pt of feesOwner: finance

Long 0% plans have the merchant pay the financing cost, which works like a hidden discount of several percent. Keep 0% for short terms and offer interest-bearing plans for longer ones. The 1.5% average assumed here is a guess; pull the actual merchant fees from Cherry before setting the terms.

R4. Kit: three fixes

+$165 per family, plus scope review+1.1 ptsOwner: operations and clinical lead
  1. Ship the devices when the refund window closes, or make the kit non-refundable once it ships. Families who leave early stop keeping $1,045 of hardware. Value: about $65 per family (8% loss down to 2%).
  2. Get volume pricing on the panel, both handhelds and the SaeboStim. These four are $935 of the $1,045. A 10% cut is about $100.
  3. Ask the clinical team whether every family needs the green panel, the green handheld and the red handheld. Dropping one handheld saves $120 to $247. This is a clinical call, not a pricing one.

R5. Report labs, physician time and supplements as pass-through

+4 pts reported margin, $0 profitOwner: finance

Labs earn cost plus 10%, and the physician is priced at cost. Bundled inside program prices, they pull the reported gross margin down: Clinical 60 shows 59.7% with them and 63.9% without. Investors adjust for pass-through anyway. Report net revenue, or show labs as a separate line, so the deck shows the margin the business actually earns. Commission already excludes them under amendment A1.

R6. Rebuild the Pre-Intensive

+$1,490 per family31% → 58% marginOwner: leadership and clinical lead

Today it sells for $5,400 against $3,708 of cost, almost all of it labs, the physician hour and supplements, and leaves $1,095 before marketing. At a $1,000 ad cost per family, it barely breaks even.

The change has three parts:

  • Price it at $5,900, with the Gut Zoomer and Organic Acids included.
  • Sell Total Tox and Neural Zoomer Plus as add-ons at retail, when the clinician indicates them.
  • Apply the kit and financing fixes.

That leaves $2,585 (44%) before marketing.

If the Pre-Intensive's real job is to feed Clinical enrolments, add a credit of the program fee toward Clinical when the family enrols within 30 days. Track the conversion rate: if fewer than about a third convert, the credit costs more than it brings in.

R7. Continuing Care: raise the coordinator rate and sell it as a membership

+$210 per quarter, recurring52% → 59% marginOwner: leadership

Coordinator sessions sell at $100 an hour against $40 cost, plus employment cost if W-2. That is the thinnest margin on any service line, and on the line investors value most. Raise the rate to $120 an hour; a 12-session quarter with one PT assessment goes from $1,440 to $1,680. Then sell Continuing Care as an auto-renewing quarterly membership offered at the closing call. The renewal rate is the number that most moves the company's valuation.

R8. Fix the cost record before the board sees the margins

AccuracyOwner: finance
  • AdrenaCalm sells at $50 against a recorded cost of $65. Either reprice it or check the invoice.
  • Several costs are carried at $0: the Hub, Eva support, Dr. Kyle, and the coach assigned to Clinical. Each makes margins look better than they are, and Eva's 100% margin is not real.
  • Kit shipping and kits lost on early exit are not in the model.

What not to do

  • Discount to close. On Clinical, a 10% discount wipes out about a fifth of what's left per family, and nearly 30% of what's left after a $2,000 ad cost.
  • Add more to the kit as a sales sweetener. Each device is pure cost, plus shipping and loss.
  • Raise commission above the amended plan. Every point comes out of what you can spend on ads.
  • Cut Clinical PT time further than R2. That erodes the reason Clinical costs more than Guided.

Guided still falls short of 20% after the package. The same $1,045 kit and $950 of labs as Clinical sit on a lower price, so they take a bigger share of it. The next levers are a smaller Guided kit (a clinical call) or a further price step on the 5-day options.

The EBITDA column treats marketing as a share of revenue. In practice ad cost is a fixed dollar amount per family, so it weighs most on the cheapest programs: $2,000 is 14% of Clinical 60 but 18% of Guided 4-day 60. That is one more reason not to discount the entry-level programs.

If close rates drop by less than these amounts, the increase makes more money. A 6% increase on a five-figure, outcome-driven purchase that is often financed usually moves close rates less than that, but there is no NeuroSage data to prove it, which is why R1 starts with a test.
